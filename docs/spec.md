# rose: product and technical specification

Version 1.2, 2026-11-04 (after day-1 de-risking). This document is the source of truth for what rose is and what "done" means. Every claim about an external system (address, version, gas figure, API) must be re-verified live before it is relied on in code; figures marked "measured" come from testnet reads on 2026-11-03 and from the day-1 spikes of 2026-11-04, recorded with their commands in `docs/verification.md`.

## 0. One line

rose runs sealed, uniform-price liquidity rounds for locked token allocations: a project buys back its insiders' locked tokens without guessing a price, holders of the same allocation exit together without dumping, and nobody, not even the operator, can read a bid before the round closes. Positions stay where they live (Base, Solana); NEAR holds them through Chain Signatures and settles everything in one ledger.

Tagline (landing): **What was locked, rose.**

Status: testnet only (NEAR testnet, Base Sepolia, Solana devnet). Nothing touches mainnet.

## 1. Problem (evidence)

- Foundations already buy back locked allocations from their early investors, always bilaterally and at a fixed, unpublished price: Omni Foundation (6.77% of supply, May 2025), LayerZero Foundation (5% of supply, Sep 2025), Ethena Foundation (Aug 2026), Monad Foundation (up to $60M, Aug 2026). Sources: https://www.nomina.io/blog/omni-foundation-repurchase, https://unchainedcrypto.com/layerzero-foundation-buys-back-5-of-zro-token-supply-from-early-investors/, https://www.kucoin.com/blog/ethena-ena-tokenomics-buyback-vc-unlocks, https://monad.xyz/blog/early-investor-liquidity-program
- A fixed price fails because holders value the lock differently: Monad's offer was declined by "nearly all" approached holders (Monad's own program page above, and https://tokenomist.ai/research/monad-tokenomics-60m-exit-offer). A single price only finds the few sellers whose reservation price is below it.
- Open-market buybacks are large ($638M in Jan-Aug 2026, https://crypto.news/crypto-token-buybacks-hit-record-638m-in-2026/) and criticized for being predictable (front-run), for insiders selling into them, and for thin books (Lido planned ~$20M against ~$90k of on-chain depth at +/-2%, https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/03/30/lido-dao-proposes-usd20-million-ldo-buyback-to-boost-price-after-95-slide).
- Corporate finance solved this decades ago with the modified Dutch auction tender offer: holders submit sealed asks, the company pays one uniform price. Reported median premium 12.6% vs 16.0% for fixed-price offers (Comment and Jarrell, 1991, as cited by later studies). No live sealed tender exists in crypto (Sidelot has announced "Sealed Offer Rounds" for memecoin OTC, not live, not on NEAR).
- Locked tokens trade OTC at 20-40% typical discounts (STIX, $200-400M per month) and up to 70%, bilaterally, with no public price (https://alearesearch.io/podcast-digest/blockworks/otc-arbitrage-and-the-hidden-yield-in-cryptos-secondary-markets-or-das-nyc-2026-or-day-3-or-insights). 90% of 16,000+ unlocks are followed by a price decline starting ~30 days before (https://keyrock.com/from-locked-to-liquidity-what-16000-token-unlocks-teach-us/).

## 2. Users and jobs

| User | Job | Today | With rose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Issuer (foundation, project treasury, DAO) | Buy back locked or vesting allocations before a cliff, fairly and verifiably | Private OTC offers at a guessed price, investor by investor | Launches a Tender round: public budget and max price, sealed asks, one uniform price, a public report |
| Holder (VC, angel, KOL round, sale participant, ex-team with a transferable position) | Get liquidity on a locked position without dumping at unlock | OTC desk at 20-70% discount, or wait and sell into the unlock | Deposits the position once, then sells in Tender or Exit rounds, or sells an Advance on its first unlocks |
| Buyer (fund, OTC desk, long-term believer) | Buy locked supply at a discount and hold the lock | Bilateral OTC, counterparty and settlement risk | Bids sealed in Exit and Advance rounds, receives units that inherit the exact schedule, claims unlocked tokens |

Primary user for the product and the demo: the issuer. Holders are the supply side. Buyers are the demand side of secondary rounds.

Most real team vestings are not transferable, so the primary onboarding path is the issuer path: the issuer calls RoseVault `createStreams(...)`, which pulls the tokens and creates the cohort's Sablier LL streams directly in custody in one transaction (non-cancelable, transferable, recipient = vault), emitting one `StreamDeposited` per stream with the NEAR account that receives the units (a stream created to the vault by a third party would carry no attribution). The issuer then distributes units to holders with `engine.distribute_units(token_id, receivers)`, a bounded batch that emits NEP-245 events (NEP-245 `mt_batch_transfer` has a single receiver, so a dedicated method is needed). Holder self-deposit of an existing transferable position is the second path.

## 3. Why blockchain, why NEAR (only load-bearing reasons)

1. **Delivery versus payment across chains without an escrow agent.** A seller is paid only if the position actually changes hands, and the position lives on Base or Solana. rose's NEAR contract controls a vault on each chain through Chain Signatures and proves foreign state through the MPC network's foreign transaction verification. On EVM-only stacks this needs a custodian or a bridge.
2. **Bids nobody can read early, including the operator.** Bids are encrypted to an identity whose key the NEAR MPC network releases (Confidential Key Derivation, domain 2) only when the round contract asks for it, and the contract can only ask after the deadline.
3. **A public, verifiable result.** Clearing price, allocations and fills are computed on-chain from opened bids. An issuer buying from its own insiders can show its community exactly what happened.
4. **One ledger for positions on several chains.** Units of a position class are NEP-245 multi-tokens on NEAR, transferable and composable, while the underlying stream never moves.

Where blockchain adds nothing, and we do not pretend otherwise: finding buyers (a cold-start problem), and the clearing algorithm itself (it runs fine off-chain in TradFi).

## 4. Product concepts

- **Class**: a set of positions that are economically identical: same chain, same protocol, same token, same normalized unlock schedule, non-cancelable. Class key (also the NEP-245 token id): lowercase hex SHA-256 of the Borsh encoding of (chain, token address or mint, decimals, normalized schedule), a Base token address in its canonical spelling (below). Normalized schedule: for Sablier, `(start, cliff, end, granularity)` kept as attested and `(deposited, startUnlock, cliffUnlock)` divided by their gcd; for Streamflow, the effective start (`cliff` when non-zero, else `start_time`, the only start the program's math reads), `end_time` and `period` kept, and `(net_amount_deposited, cliff_amount, amount_per_period)` divided by their gcd. Usually one cohort of one sale (same start, cliff, end). Positions in a class are fungible, which is what allows N sellers against M buyers at one price.
- **Position**: one locked allocation on a foreign chain, held by rose's vault. Supported in v1: Sablier Lockup v4 streams on Base Sepolia (ERC-721, Lockup Linear model only, any `granularity` (seconds per unlock step; v4 stores at least 1 and the unlock function models it), must be created `transferable = true`, non-cancelable or renounced, not canceled, not depleted, and still holding at least one whole token (`10^decimals` base units) after the deposit-time withdrawal; Dynamic, Tranched and Price-Gated streams are refused) and Streamflow streams on Solana devnet (`transfer_recipient` allowed for the recipient; `cancelable_by_sender`, `transferable_by_sender`, `pausable`, `can_update_rate` and `can_topup` all 0, `closed` 0, `canceled_at` 0, classic SPL Token mint). Cancelable positions are rejected.
- **Unit**: one allocation unit of a class, i.e. a claim on one base unit of the class token under the class schedule. A holder who deposits a position with allocation `D` (in base units) receives `D` units. Units are NEP-245 tokens, token id = class id. Holding units or cash requires a NEP-145 storage registration on the engine (`storage_deposit`, minimum = the account record, one holding row and one cash row, 619 bytes or 0.00619 NEAR; each further class held, and each further cash venue with a balance, costs one more row). Rows are charged at their measured size for the longest account id (64 characters: record 137 bytes, holding row 310 bytes including the class's entry in the account's class list, cash row 172 bytes, lien share row 149 bytes paid from the funder's bid deposit), so the engine never pays for a row it charged, whatever the id's length. A voluntary receipt (`mt_transfer`, `mt_transfer_call`, `distribute_units`, the mint of a deposit the holder requested, a NEAR cash deposit through `ft_transfer_call`, which the token refunds when refused) is refused unless the receiver is registered with capacity for the row; a Base cash registration pays the credited account's cash row (and its registration, if it has none) out of its record deposit, and a Base cash token outside the accepted list also records its token in the account's list of such venues (159 bytes more, charged with the row, so the cash view shows every balance and an unregistration that is refused names what the account still holds); an Exit buyer must have room for the class's holding row when it bids, and the bid reserves those bytes until it settles (units of another class received meanwhile need room of their own); while everything the engine owes an account (settled units, returned locks, refunds, lien releases, `mt_transfer_call` refunds) always lands, the engine paying a missing row (an owed credit to an account that is not registered registers it with a zero deposit, so its rows stay accounted for). An account cannot withdraw or unregister its storage while it has live bids, locked units, liens or custody actions in flight, so an owed credit never finds an unfunded account, and `storage_withdraw` always leaves the registration minimum (`storage_balance_bounds().min`), so a registered account can always receive one class.
- **Unlock function**: each class has a deterministic `f(t)` in [0, 1], the fraction of the allocation unlocked at time `t`, stored as fixed point with 1e18 precision. It is an exact port of the protocol's own math over the attested schedule: for Sablier, `LockupMath.calculateStreamedAmountLL` of `lockup@v4.0.1` (start unlock at `start`, cliff unlock at `cliff`, then a linear segment floored to whole multiples of `granularity`, plain integer arithmetic, not UD60x18), lowered on the linear segment by a margin of `ceil(1e18 / 10^decimals)` because Sablier floors per stream and that floor does not scale across members (this is why a position must hold at least one whole token); for Streamflow, `cliff_amount + floor((t - start) / period) * amount_per_period` capped at the deposit, with no margin because it is exactly proportional. `f` is a lower bound: `D_p * f(t) <= 1e18 * streamed_p(t)` for every member `p` of the class (property-tested, and the Sablier port matches 197 of 197 live `streamedAmountOf` reads). A unit's accrued entitlement between two times is `f(t2) - f(t1)` tokens. Locked tokens per unit at time `t` = `1 - f(t)`.
- **Accrual accounting**: every account holds `(balance, checkpoint, claimable, carry)` per class. On every balance change (mint, `mt_transfer`, `mt_transfer_call` refund, lock into a round, settlement, burn) the contract first accrues `balance * (f(now) - checkpoint)` into `claimable` and sets `checkpoint = f(now)`; the fractional part of each accrual (below one base unit) is carried into the next one, so a holding's lifetime accrual is the floor of its exact accrual, never a sum of floors (a holder who kept its units has exactly `floor(units * (f - checkpoint))`, which is what an in-kind redeem computes in one step, 7.3). Units locked in a round keep accruing to the seller until `ts = closes_at`; sold units accrue to the buyer from `ts`; unsold units return to the seller with their accrual. All amounts use 256-bit arithmetic; rounding always favors the pool (dust stays in the vault).
- **Round**: a sealed auction over units of one class. Three types:
  - **Tender**: one public buyer (the issuer) with a public budget and a public max price; sealed asks from any number of holders. Units bought are retired (their unlocked tokens go to the issuer's chosen address or a burn address).
  - **Exit**: sealed asks from any number of holders of the class, sealed bids from any number of buyers. Uniform price at the intersection.
  - **Advance**: one holder sells a senior claim on the first `K` tokens that will unlock from `X` of their units, with `K <= X * (1 - f(ts))`; sealed bids from funders. Winners receive lien shares; accruals of the encumbered units flow to lien holders until `K` tokens are delivered, then the encumbrance ends. Encumbered units are non-transferable and cannot enter other rounds. No oracle, no liquidation.
- **Clearing prices**: every settled round publishes its clearing price per locked token, its remaining lock duration and its volume. The Prices page shows the history per class and price versus remaining lock time across classes of the same token. There is no spot oracle on testnet, so no implied discount is computed on-chain; when an issuer declares a public reference price for a Tender, the page shows the discount to that declared reference, labeled as such.

## 5. Mechanism

### 5.1 Sealing

- Scheme: Boneh-Franklin IBE over BLS12-381 against the NEAR MPC CKD domain 2 key (identity on G1, master key on G2), as implemented and proven on testnet by Sealwell (`Godwin-web3/sealwell`, MIT OR Apache-2.0). We depend on or port its `sealwell-ibe` crate with attribution (README "Third-party code" section). The same crate compiles to wasm for the browser and to the NEAR contract, so encryption and verification share one implementation.
- Identity per round: `app_id = SHA3-256("near-mpc v0.1.0 app_id derivation:" || engine_account || "," || "round/<id>")`. The contract derives the path from the round id, never from an argument.
- Plaintext: `(quantity: u128, limit_price: u128, salt: [u8; 16])`, bound to `(round_id, bidder, side)` as associated data.
- The key request (`request_app_private_key` on `v1.signer-prod.testnet`, `AppPublicKeyPV` variant with a public scalar, 1 yocto) exists in exactly one function, callable by anyone, guarded by `require!(now >= round.closes_at)`. Gas (measured): the live config requires 15 Tgas attached and reserves 7 Tgas for the yield return, and the request itself burns about 11.6 Tgas, so 15 to 18 Tgas fail with "Exceeded the prepaid gas"; the measured minimum is 19 Tgas and the engine attaches 25. The callback (unwrap of `big_c - a * big_y`, then the pairing check) costs 12.4 Tgas and gets a fixed 20 Tgas (margin over the measurement, so a slower host never starves the callback that stores the key); the app attaches 80 Tgas to `close`. Retries are unbounded (the key is deterministic). `submit_key(sk)` is open to anyone and verified with `bls12381_pairing_check` (10.2 Tgas), so a lost callback can never freeze a round: the MPC response (`big_y`, `big_c`) is public in the receipt and anyone can unwrap it with the public scalar. Untrusted BLS points never rely on host decompression for subgroup checks (NEP-488 `bls12381_p1/p2_decompress` do not check membership); they go through `g1_multiexp` or `pairing_check`.
- Measured on testnet (rose spike, consistent with Sealwell's 4.3 to 6.1 s): 4.2 to 4.8 s from the close transaction to the stored key, 35.0 Tgas in total including the callback; 12 to 17 ms per encryption in the browser, 192-byte ciphertexts.

### 5.2 Opening (fully verified, no keeper, no challenge game)

1. Once the key is public, anyone can open any bid by submitting `(plaintext, sigma)`. The contract fully verifies the opening: it recomputes `V` from the public key with one pairing in wasm and checks `U`, `V` and `W` (Sealwell measured 119.8 Tgas, 124 Tgas per receipt; rose measured 124.3 to 124.4 Tgas per transaction, 0.0124 NEAR), so each opening fits one transaction under the 300 Tgas wallet cap. A ciphertext with an inconsistent `V` can therefore be opened by nobody, its author included (shown live: the author's own sigma and plaintext fail on chain while the key is public): there is no free option to reveal or withhold after seeing the book, and no challenge window. A ciphertext copied from another bidder fails at the `U` check (4.4 Tgas) because the associated data binds `(round_id, bidder, side)`.
2. The key being public, every participant's browser can decrypt every bid locally; the app opens all remaining bids in sequence for whoever clicks "Finalize" (each opening is a separate transaction prepaid with 200 Tgas, about 1.5 times the 132 Tgas the staging engine burns, because prepaid gas is charged up front at the pessimistic gas price and 300 Tgas would require 0.30 NEAR of balance from a small demo account; the opener pays about 0.0132 NEAR of gas per bid).
3. After the reveal window, bids that nobody opened (malformed) are void and their bond is slashed. An opened bid is also void (and slashed) if a buyer's `limit * quantity` exceeds its escrow, a buyer's quantity exceeds the round's `max_buy_quantity` (fixed at creation and shown with the round: the class supply then in an Exit, the claim in an Advance; fixed so that a redeem after the bid was sealed, which shrinks the supply, never voids a covered bid, and so that the clearing sums stay bounded), or a seller's quantity exceeds its locked units.
4. Known property (ePrint 2025/2048): anything encrypted to a round identity, including ciphertexts that were never included, is readable after close. Documented in the security model.

### 5.3 Collateral and what stays hidden

- Buyers escrow a public budget (cash) at bid time; the sealed bid holds their limit price and quantity. Sellers lock units into the round at ask time (publicly, because positions are public anyway); the sealed ask holds their minimum price and the quantity they actually sell (<= locked).
- Hidden until close: every price and every exact quantity. Public: number of bids, each bid's escrow or locked amount.
- Each bid posts a bond in the round's cash token, refunded on a valid opening: max(1 USDC, 1% of the bid's escrow or of its locked units valued at the round's public max price).

### 5.4 Clearing (computed on-chain, bounded)

Prices are quoted per locked token at the round's settlement time `ts = closes_at`; the contract converts to per-unit using `1 - f(ts)`. Ties are resolved deterministically; ticks of 1e-6 USDC.

- **Tender**: candidate prices are the submitted asks `<= max_price`. Supply `S(P)` = sum of quantities with ask `<= P`. Volume bought at `P` is `Q(P) = min(S(P), floor(budget / P))`. `P*` = argmax of `Q` over candidates, ties to the lowest `P`. Asks are filled in ascending order of price; the marginal price level is filled pro rata; every filled seller receives `P*`; the unspent budget is refunded. Example: asks 100 @ 9 and 10 @ 10, budget 950: `Q(9) = 100`, `Q(10) = 95`, so `P* = 9`, 100 units bought for 900, 50 refunded. Property-tested against a brute-force reference.
- **Exit**: demand `D(P)` = sum of bid quantities with limit `>= P`, supply `S(P)` as above, volume `V(P) = min(D, S)`. `P*` maximizes `V`; ties broken by minimal `|D - S|`, then by the midpoint of the tie interval (which is contiguous, since `D` is non-increasing and `S` non-decreasing) rounded down to a tick. Fill rule (as implemented in `contracts/engine/src/clearing/uniform.rs`): the short side fills fully; on the long side, orders are taken by price priority (bids by descending limit, asks by ascending minimum, then ascending id) in whole price levels while the cumulative quantity fits `V(P*)`; the first level that does not fit is rationed pro rata by largest remainder (ties to the lower id); every level below it is unfilled. Because `P*` usually lies strictly inside the tie interval where no order sits, "strictly better limits fill fully" holds level by level, not by comparison with `P*`: the rationed level may be above `P*`. Unfilled buyer escrow is refundable.
- **Advance**: the holder's sealed reserve price is one ask for the `K`-token claim; funders' bids are per claimed token. Same price and fill rule as Exit with a single ask, partial fill allowed down to a holder-chosen minimum (`min_fill <= K`; below it, no crossing). When demand is short the price follows the Exit rule literally: the midpoint of the tie interval, possibly above the holder's reserve (one bid of 50 @ 20 against a reserve of 10 clears at 15). This is intended: the reserve is a floor, not a price, and the midpoint shares the surplus between the holder and the funders.
- An opened, non-void order priced above the round's `max_price` (a buyer's limit or a seller's minimum) takes no part in the clearing: it is neither filled nor void, and its bond, escrow and locked units come back at settlement as for any unfilled order. The order form refuses such a price.
- A round cannot be created on a class that is fully unlocked at `ts` (`f(ts) = 1`): there is no locked token to price.
- Settlement: `clear(round)` computes `P*` and every fill in one bounded transaction (a round accepts at most `MAX_BIDS_PER_ROUND = 64` bids, so `clear` always fits), then `settle_bid(round, bid)` applies one bid's result at a time (idempotent, callable by anyone): units or lien shares to buyers, cash to sellers, refunds, bond refund or slash, storage deposit refund. This per-bid settlement is the pagination; there is no multi-page `clear`. Units and cash move inside the NEAR ledger; payouts to foreign chains are pull-based (section 7). A round with no crossing is `Cancelled` and everything unlocks.
- Cash venue: each round declares exactly one cash venue at creation (NEAR tUSDC, the rose NEP-141 at `tusdc.rose-app.testnet`, or a Base ERC-20 held by RoseVault from the engine's accepted list: rose tUSDC and Circle USDC `0x036CbD53842c5426634e7929541eC2318f3dCF7e`). All escrows, bonds, fees and seller proceeds of that round live in that venue and are claimable only there. The bid form shows the venue.

### 5.5 Fees

30 bps of settled notional, charged to the buying side on top of the price, shown before every bid and in every receipt. The issuer in a Tender escrows `budget + 30 bps of budget` at creation; the fee actually charged is 30 bps of the notional actually bought, so at settlement the unspent budget and the unused part of the fee are both refunded. Exit and Advance buyers escrow `limit * quantity` plus its fee and get back everything above their charge plus fee. This undercuts OTC desk spreads by an order of magnitude. Fee recipient is fixed at init. Slashed bonds go to the same treasury.

### 5.6 Round lifecycle (every transition is a public method; there is no keeper)

`Scheduled -> Open -> KeyRequested -> Revealing -> Clearing -> Settled | Cancelled`

- Creation bounds every caller-chosen duration (`get_limits` reports them): `closes_at` in the future and at most 30 days after creation, `key_timeout` between 1 minute and 7 days, `reveal_window` between 5 minutes and 7 days. Without them any creator (an Exit is open to anyone) could freeze a round forever (`clear` waits for the end of the reveal window) or slash every bond (a reveal window shorter than the time it takes to land an opening). The minimum key timeout leaves time for a `close` sent right after `closes_at` (about 5 s to the key); the minimum reveal window leaves time to open a full book of 64 orders.
- `Open`: bids and asks accepted until `closes_at` (block timestamp), at most `MAX_BIDS_PER_ROUND = 64` per round. A round can be cancelled by its creator only before its first bid; never after `closes_at`. Exit rounds can be created by anyone (`create_exit`); a Tender by its issuer; an Advance by the holder.
- `close(round)`: anyone, after `closes_at`; requests the key (retries unbounded until `closes_at + key_timeout`).
- `KeyRequested`: ends when the key arrives (callback or `submit_key`). If no key has arrived by `closes_at + key_timeout` (default 1 h), anyone can call `cancel_no_key(round)` once no key request is in flight (250 blocks after the last `close`, past the MPC's 200-block yield, so the engine can never store a key, nor open or clear anything, for a cancelled round): the round is `Cancelled` and every escrow, bond and locked unit is refundable. The key itself may still become public: MPC nodes that answer after the yield timeout publish `(big_y, big_c)` in their failed `respond_ckd` transaction, which anyone unwraps with the round's public scalar, and a response whose callback failed is public while the round waits in `KeyRequested`. The sealed orders of a cancelled round can therefore become readable after the fact. No funds move after `Cancelled`, so the consequence is privacy only; it is the same property as 5.2 point 4 (ePrint 2025/2048) and the security model states it.
- `Revealing`: starts at `key_received_at`, lasts `reveal_window` (default 10 min in the demo, 24 h suggested in production). Openings are accepted during the window and the app opens everything automatically.
- `clear(round)`: anyone, after the window; computes `P*` and every fill in one bounded transaction (section 5.4) and sets `Settled` or `Cancelled`.
- `settle_bid(round, bid)`: anyone, idempotent; applies one bid's result (the pagination of settlement).
- Claims: each participant pulls their cash, units or refunds.

The UI shows the next available action to whoever is looking at the round ("Close round", "Finalize", "Claim"), so the people who care about the outcome advance it themselves. The contract keeps a bounded, paginated event history in state so the app needs no indexer.

## 7. Cross-chain flows

### 7.1 Deposit a Sablier position (Base Sepolia)

1. Holder connects an EVM wallet (or the in-app demo wallet), the app lists their Sablier v4 streams and flags each as eligible (transferable, non-cancelable or renounced, not canceled, not depleted, at least one whole token left to lock, token and schedule matching an existing class or creatable as a new class).
2. Holder approves RoseVault for the stream NFT, then calls `depositStream` (payable: `calculateMinFeeWei(streamId)` plus a margin, the unused part is refunded). RoseVault withdraws any vested balance to the holder, takes the NFT, and emits `StreamDeposited(sender, nearAccount, lockup, streamId, token, decimals, deposited, withdrawn, start, cliff, end, granularity, startUnlock, cliffUnlock, cancelable, transferable, timestamp)` (`sender` and `streamId` indexed; `decimals` read on-chain with `IERC20Metadata.decimals()` because the engine needs it for the class key and the margin and must not trust the caller; `withdrawn` includes the amount this deposit just withdrew; `cancelable` is always false and `transferable` always true because the vault refuses anything else, and both are emitted so the engine checks them). `StreamAttested` carries the same `decimals` field after `token`. The issuer path (`createStreams`) emits the same event per stream.
3. The app waits until Base's `safe` head has passed the receipt's block (observed about 3 min after mining, head jumps every 3 to 4 min) plus about 30 s of margin because the MPC nodes' own providers can lag the public RPC, then calls `engine.register_base_deposit(tx_hash, block_hash, block_number, transaction_index, log_index, values)`. The engine requests `verify_foreign_transaction` (domain 3, Base, `Log` extractor, finality `Safe`, exactly 15 Tgas attached (the live floor) plus 1 yocto, a fixed 20 Tgas callback; attach 80 Tgas: after its own work the call keeps 50 Tgas for the request and its callback, so 50 attached fails) **without** `expected_payload_hash`: measured, a mismatch with that field is silent and times out after 200 blocks (about 2 min), whereas without it the nodes sign the true values in about 7 s and the engine detects the mismatch itself. In the callback the engine recomputes `sha256(borsh(ForeignTxSignPayload::V1 { request, values }))` from the caller-supplied `EvmLog { removed = false, log_index, transaction_index, transaction_hash, block_hash, block_number, address, data, topics }` (`log_index` is the receipt's block-level `logIndex`, not the position within the transaction; `data` is the RPC string, `0x` plus lowercase hex) and compares it with the response's `payload_hash`; then it checks emitter = RoseVault, lockup = canonical Sablier, non-cancelable, transferable, schedule = class schedule (built from the attested `decimals`, never from a caller-supplied value), and mints `D = deposited` units (the full allocation of the stream) to `nearAccount` with checkpoint `f(timestamp)`, raised to the withdrawn fraction `ceil(1e18 * withdrawn / deposited)` and capped at `f(now)` (`deposit_units_of`: the class fraction is lowered by the margin of section 4, so on its own it would let the units claim up to one base unit per whole token more than the stream pays). In the rare case where the withdrawn fraction is still above `f(now)` at registration, the units are reduced instead to the largest count whose lifetime claim fits `deposited - withdrawn` (property-tested, as for Solana in 7.2). `D` is the full allocation and not `deposited - withdrawn`: the already-vested part was withdrawn to the holder before the deposit, and the checkpoint is what excludes it from accrual. With 100 deposited and 40 already vested, `D = 100` units at checkpoint 0.4 accrue the 60 tokens the stream will pay into the vault; `D = 60` would accrue only 36 and orphan 24. Every EVM address the engine receives (log fields, payout recipients, venue tokens, a Tender's `retire_to`, the init configuration) is canonicalized to `0x` plus 40 lowercase hex characters before it is hashed, stored, compared or used as a key, so a bare or mixed-case spelling behaves exactly like the canonical one: a third party who registers someone else's log with another spelling registers exactly the position or cash the canonical spelling would, and cannot split it into a venue or class nobody can use. Replay key `(chain, tx_hash, log_index)`: marked pending when the request goes out, cleared on `PromiseError` or mismatch, consumed only on a match. Every MPC-side failure (wrong values, unknown transaction, wrong log index, finality not reached) surfaces as one `PromiseError` after about 2 min, so the UI shows "verification timed out, retry" with those causes; retries are user-initiated. Measured round trip on success: 6 to 9 s, 8 to 13 NEAR blocks.

### 7.2 Deposit a Streamflow position (Solana devnet)

The deposit is three engine calls: attribute, move, register. A deposit address exists per `(holder, class)` so that the engine can prove whose deposit a stream is; the proof is taken before anything is recorded, because a request-only move record could be front-run (the engine would sign a useless move for an attacker, and `transfer_recipient` leaves no trace of the previous recipient, so a later registration could not tell whose deposit address the stream came from).

1. The app computes the class id (`compute_sol_class_id(mint, decimals, schedule)`, a pure view: the class need not exist yet, the first deposit of a class creates it at registration) and derives `dep/<near_account>/<class>` (Ed25519 via `derived_public_key`); the holder calls `transfer_recipient` to it from Phantom (or the demo wallet).
2. `engine.attribute_sol_deposit(stream, class_id, return_to, anchor_tx, metadata_data, mint_data)` (0.015 NEAR of record storage, returned when nothing is recorded; attach 120 Tgas: after its own work the call keeps 80 Tgas for the key view, the proof and their callbacks, so 80 attached fails, checked in the sandbox; 27 Tgas burnt live): the engine derives the deposit address itself (`derived_public_key` of `dep/<caller>/<class_id>`), requires `recipient == deposit address` and proves the stream metadata and its mint account with one ForeignTx `AccountState` request at `Finalized` (two extractors, owners Streamflow program and SPL Token program; Token-2022 mints are refused). An eligible stream (flags below, a mint without a freeze authority, at least one whole token left to lock, class not fully unlocked, `end_time` more than one hour away: Streamflow's auto-claim, 7.3, would otherwise empty a stream still at its deposit address, or refused at the vault, into that address's token account, where no engine path reaches it) is recorded as attributed to the caller with the class recomputed from the attested metadata; an ineligible one is recorded as refused at the deposit address and returnable to `return_to` (`return_refused_deposit`, signed by the deposit key). The callback re-checks the stream's stage and never touches its replay key: a stream already moved, registered (released since included: one registration per stream, ever) or refused at the vault, or with a registration in flight, is never attributed again. Cost: one ForeignTx round trip (about 7 s). A mint with a freeze authority (the 82-byte mint's `freeze_authority` `COption` at offset 46: `u32` tag, then the key at 50..82, verified on devnet) is refused here: whoever holds it could freeze the vault's token account, and every Streamflow withdrawal into it, so every payout of the class, would fail while the tokens stay in escrow. A mint without one can never get one (`spl-token` `SetAuthority` fails with `MintCannotFreeze`).
3. `engine.move_sol_deposit(stream, recent_blockhash)` (the payout storage deposit, exactly 0.025 NEAR; attach 120 Tgas: the call keeps 70 Tgas for its joined signatures and their callback, so 70 attached fails, checked in the sandbox; 29 Tgas burnt live): the engine builds and signs the message itself: `create_associated_token_account_idempotent(payer = gas/sol, ATA(vault, mint))` then Streamflow `transfer_recipient` from the deposit address to the vault (the program would otherwise charge the ATA rent to the deposit address, which holds no SOL; `gas/sol` pays that rent once per mint), fee payer = `gas/sol`, two joined `sign` promises (`dep/...` and `gas/sol`). The caller supplies a recent blockhash rather than a durable nonce: nothing is debited by this call, so a wrong or expired blockhash only means a retry (callable again while the stream is not registered), and it avoids one nonce account per deposit (MPC `sign` median 4.9 s fits the blockhash lifetime). Anyone broadcasts. If the stream is not at the deposit address the transaction simply fails on chain.
4. `engine.register_sol_deposit(stream, anchor_tx, account_data)` (`registration_deposit_for`: 0.015 NEAR of record storage, kept, plus the holder's storage, whose unused part is returned; attach 80 Tgas: the call keeps 50 Tgas for its proof and callback; 21 Tgas burnt live): one ForeignTx `AccountState` proof (domain 3, finality `Finalized`, 15 Tgas plus 1 yocto, no `expected_payload_hash`) on the Streamflow metadata account (1,104 bytes, little-endian, no discriminator; offsets verified on a stream created with distinctive values: `canceled_at` 25, `recipient` 113, `recipient_tokens` 145, `mint` 177, `streamflow_fee_total` 305, `streamflow_fee_withdrawn` 313, `streamflow_fee_percent` 321, `partner` 325, `partner_tokens` 357, `partner_fee_total` 389, `partner_fee_withdrawn` 397, `partner_fee_percent` 405, `start_time` 409, `net_amount_deposited` 417, `period` 425, `amount_per_period` 433, `cliff` 441, `cliff_amount` 449, `cancelable_by_sender` 457, `automatic_withdrawal` 459, `transferable_by_sender` 460, `transferable_by_recipient` 461, `can_topup` 462, `pausable` 539, `can_update_rate` 540, `closed` 671) shows owner = Streamflow program, recipient = vault, `recipient_tokens` = the vault's associated token account, mint = the attributed mint, schedule = class, at least one whole token (`10^decimals`) deposited and not yet withdrawn, `transferable_by_recipient` 1 (the vault must be able to release or return the stream), and `closed`, `canceled_at`, `cancelable_by_sender`, `transferable_by_sender`, `automatic_withdrawal`, `pausable`, `can_update_rate`, `can_topup`, the pause and rate-change history and every Streamflow and partner fee field all 0 (top-ups would change the deposit and end after registration; automatic withdrawals would move unlocked tokens outside the engine's accounting, see 7.3; a fee-free withdrawal touches neither the treasury's nor the partner's token account, so neither has to exist, verified on devnet). `AccountState` signs the account's current state as the nodes read it (slot >= the anchor transaction's slot), not the state as of the transaction, so the app reads `getAccountInfo` at `finalized` right before the call and passes those exact bytes; the anchor is the `move_sol_deposit` transaction (or any successful finalized transaction not newer than the read). If the stream changed in flight the recomputed hash differs and the call fails in about 7 s; the app retries with fresh bytes. A proof showing the stream anywhere but at the vault (the move has not executed yet) changes nothing: the key is cleared, the stage stays, everything attached is returned; only a stream proven at the vault can be refused there. Units are minted to the attributed account: `net_amount_deposited` units when the attested `amount_withdrawn / net_amount_deposited` is below `f(now)` (checkpoint = that withdrawn fraction, so what unlocked between the holder's last withdrawal and the registration accrues to the depositor), otherwise the largest unit count whose lifetime claim fits `net_amount_deposited - amount_withdrawn`; the attested `amount_withdrawn` seeds the engine's per-stream withdrawal accounting. Replay key: the stream metadata pubkey (one registration per stream). A stream refused at the vault is returnable from the vault and never attributed again (a vault-signed return must never meet a registered position).

### 7.3 Unlocked tokens and payouts

- Base: anyone calls Sablier `withdrawMax(streamId, RoseVault)` (allowed because `to == recipient`); the app does it inside the claim flow. A unit holder's claim: the engine accrues and debits the claimable balance, asks the MPC to sign an EIP-712 `Transfer(token, to, amount, id)` for RoseVault (domain 0, path `vault/base`; measured 12.7 Tgas burnt, 21 Tgas prepaid minimum, median 4.9 s), stores `(v, r, s)` in its callback; the claimant's browser submits `executeTransfer` (or `executeRelease` for an in-kind redeem) and pays the gas. The authorization is stored on NEAR and can be re-submitted at any time; RoseVault's bitmap prevents double use. If a request is still unanswered 600 blocks later (its callback starved or the MPC timed out without an error reaching the engine), anyone calls `retry_base_signature(payout_id)`: the same digest and id are signed again and nothing is debited; the first signature that arrives (or is submitted) completes the payout, any later callback changes nothing, and a failed request never refunds (a late response to any request may be public, see async safety below).
- Solana: the engine builds the message itself (never signs bytes supplied by a caller) and a payout is two-phase, because a durable-nonce transaction that fails on chain still advances its nonce and only the chain can say whether it executed. `claim_sol_tokens(source, draws, swept?, to_owner, nonce)` (0.025 NEAR; attach 200 Tgas: the call keeps 105 Tgas for its proof, the joined signatures and their callbacks; 45 Tgas burnt live): the claimant names the streams of the class to draw on (`draws`, each at most `unlocked(now) - engine_withdrawn - pending_withdraw` of that stream as `get_sol_streams` reports it, at least one whole token unless the draw empties the stream entirely; streams still held by the vault, not releasing, with no unconfirmed payout, and more than five minutes before their `end_time`) and an amount of the class's swept balance (`swept`, below), and the claim is their sum. The message is `AdvanceNonceAccount` on the claimant's durable nonce account (80 bytes, authority = vault, rent read live with `getMinimumBalanceForRentExemption(80)`, 1,056,640 lamports today; created from the claimant's wallet with a seed, `createNonceAccount({ basePubkey, seed })`, whose address `sha256(base || seed || System program)` the engine recomputes: the seed starts with the first 24 hex characters of `sha256(caller's NEAR account id)` (`nonce_seed_prefix_of`) and may add up to 8 characters, so a nonce account serves only the NEAR account its seed names and nobody can spend, or front-run, another claimant's nonce values; a wallet can hold several, and a nonce account is reused across claims since its value changes after each), one Streamflow `withdraw(amount_s)` per drawn stream (accounts as its metadata names them), then SPL `transfer_checked` of the sum from the vault's token account to the claimant's existing associated token account, then `AdvanceNonceAccount` on the claimant's receipt nonce account (a second nonce account of the claimant, same base and seed rule, authority = vault), fee payer = `gas/sol`: the vault's token account receives in the same transaction what the streams pay, and the only balance outside the streams it relies on is the swept balance, proven by `sync_sol_stream` (below), and `gas/sol` pays fees only (no account creation; it pays account rent only in a deposit move, once per mint, and in the return of a refused deposit, for the return address's token account). Before signing, the nonce account and the receipt account (owner System, initialized, authority at bytes 8..40 = vault, nonce value at 40..72, two distinct accounts, neither held by another unsettled action) and the recipient's token account (owner SPL Token, initialized, not frozen, mint and owner as expected; 165 bytes) are proven together with one ForeignTx `AccountState` request, so a missing or unusable recipient account fails before anything is signed. The two signatures (`vault/sol`, `gas/sol`) are requested as joined `sign` promises on domain 1 and verified against the derived keys; the transaction (wire format `[2] || sig(gas) || sig(vault) || message`) is stored on NEAR so anyone can broadcast it, at any time (durable nonce, fee paid by `gas/sol`), and re-broadcast it while unconfirmed. The payout stays `Signed`, each drawn stream carries it as its one unconfirmed payout, and its two nonce accounts stay reserved to it, until anyone calls `confirm_sol_payout(payout, anchor_tx, nonce_data, receipt_data)`: one ForeignTx request proves the nonce account's value changed from the signed one (the transaction can no longer execute) and the receipt account. The verdict reads those two accounts only, because a durable-nonce transaction that fails on chain still advances its nonce but rolls back every other instruction, the receipt's advance included, while one that executes leaves both accounts on the same new value, derived from the blockhash of the block it executed in (both measured on devnet, `docs/verification.md`): the receipt equal to the nonce's new value proves the transaction executed, the payout is `Landed` and each draw counts in `engine_withdrawn` (a Streamflow `withdraw(amount)` withdraws exactly `amount` or fails, error 115 "Amount requested is larger than available", measured); the receipt behind it proves the transaction failed or its cancellation executed, and the claim is refunded on that verdict only from two such proofs, the second requested at least 150 blocks after the first one was answered (the nodes read each account separately, and a lagging read of the receipt right after a landing must not pass for "did not execute"; measuring from the first answer to the second request, not between the two answers, guarantees the two reads are that far apart). Why this witness and not the streams or the token accounts: Streamflow's auto-claim (below) empties a stream at its end whether or not a payout drew on it, so a stream's `amount_withdrawn` no longer tells; the vault's token account takes donations from anyone and is shared by every class of the mint; the recipient's token account is controlled by its owner, who could empty it to fake "did not execute". The receipt can only move by a vault signature, the engine signs its advance only inside this payout's message, and both nonce accounts are reserved to the payout (any other action naming either is refused) until it is settled, so between signature and verdict nothing else can advance either; a stale read of the receipt at request time does not matter, since the verdict compares it with the nonce's new value, not with its old value. One unconfirmed payout per stream keeps a stream's withdrawable amount and its sync simple. Message serialization follows web3.js `TransactionMessage.compileToLegacyMessage()` byte for byte; classic SPL Token mints only (Token-2022 transfer hooks are not modeled); the 1,232-byte packet bounds the draws of one payout.
- Streamflow's auto-claim and the swept balance (Solana): one second after a stream's `end_time`, Streamflow's withdrawor (`wdrwhnCv4pzW8beKsbPa4S2UDZrXenjg16KJdKSpb5u`, memo "Auto-Claim") withdraws its whole remainder into the recipient's token account and closes the escrow, even with `automatic_withdrawal` = 0 (measured on devnet: `amount_withdrawn` becomes `net_amount_deposited`, `last_withdrawn_at` becomes `end_time + 1`, `closed` becomes 1, nothing else changes; after it a `withdraw` fails with Anchor error 3012 on the closed escrow and a `transfer_recipient` with error 104, and a failed durable-nonce transaction carrying either still advances its nonce). For a vault stream those tokens land in the vault's token account outside any payout. So a stream takes no new draw and no redeem from five minutes before its end, and `sync_sol_stream(position, anchor_tx, metadata_data)` (anyone; attach 80 Tgas; one `AccountState` proof at `Finalized`) books them: when the metadata shows the stream at the vault, paying the vault's token account, with `amount_withdrawn` above `engine_withdrawn`, the difference is added to the class's swept balance (`get_sol_swept(class)`: `balance`, and `pending` for unconfirmed payouts), `engine_withdrawn` catches up, and a fully withdrawn stream is pruned. A sync is refused, and re-checked in its callback, while a payout drawing on the stream or its release is unsettled (nobody knows yet whether that transaction moved tokens); a lagging read shows less withdrawn, never more, so it books less and a later sync books the rest; it reads the stream, never the vault's token account, so a donation books nothing. The swept balance pays claims with `transfer_checked` alone (`swept` in `claim_sol_tokens`, at most `balance - pending`), settled on the receipt like any payout: landed, the balance pays it; not executed, the reservation is released. Solvency: each class's swept balance is backed by the tokens its own streams' auto-claims put in the vault's token account, only vault-signed transfers take tokens out, and a payout reserves what it takes, so the account always holds at least the sum of the classes' available swept balances. A payout signed before the end and broadcast after the auto-claim fails on the closed escrow, reads "did not execute" and is refunded; the stream, synced once it is settled, then books its whole remainder, the failed draw included. One that executed before the auto-claim lands, and the sync books only what the auto-claim took. A record of the class's swept balance is created, empty, with the class's first Solana registration, whose record deposit pays it.
- Async safety: debit before every `sign` promise; each `sign` gets 15 Tgas (the live floor; the MPC README's 10 Tgas is stale) and every callback static gas covering what it attaches, the receipt fees and its own work (every user call checks it has that much left). A signature request never refunds: a failed request (`PromiseError`) does not prove that no signature will ever surface (an MPC response arriving after the yield timeout is public in its failed transaction). A Base authorization is retried until it is signed (its id stays executable on RoseVault forever, so it must never be refunded); a Solana durable-nonce action whose authority signature failed is cancelled on chain instead: the engine signs a transaction that only advances the same nonce (at most one of the two can execute, and the cancellation leaves the receipt where it was), and the confirmation settles it, landed or refunded, whichever executed. Only a proof that failed (nothing was signed) or a confirmation that the transaction did not execute refunds. For a joined pair, if only the fee-payer signature failed, `resign_sol_gas` re-requests `gas/sol` over the same stored message. A response the callback could not read is completed by anyone with `submit_base_signature(payout, r, s, v)` (recovered against the vault address) or `submit_sol_signature(payout, signer_path, signature)` (verified against the derived key); a high-`s` secp256k1 signature is normalized, never refused. Every `Requested` state has a retry, at once after a failed request and 600 blocks after one whose callback never ran: `retry_base_signature` signs the same digest again; `retry_sol_payout` abandons and refunds a payout still waiting for its proof (nothing was signed), signs the stored message (the original or its cancellation) again in the signing phase, and marks failed a move or return whose callback never ran (its recent blockhash has expired by then); a move or return whose authority request failed is marked failed at once (nothing was debited, a late signature of it can only execute the same `transfer_recipient`, and only one `transfer_recipient` of a stream can execute), and is requested again with a fresh blockhash; an expired deposit registration may be requested again. The engine's own Solana keys (vault, fee payer) are refused as recipients and return addresses, a stream's deposit address as the `to` of its redeem, RoseVault and the engine's `vault/base` address as the `to` of every Base payout and redeem, and RoseVault, every engine key and the zero address as a Tender's `retire_to` (a retired lot paid there would sit outside every record, or could never be paid); a Tender can therefore be created only once the engine is bootstrapped and its keys are known. EVM digests are checked to be below the secp256k1 group order n before the request (the MPC rejects payloads >= n, not >= p as its README says). The engine keeps 0.5 NEAR free above its storage before issuing any MPC request.
- Cash: on NEAR, `ft_transfer` (pull) of rose tUSDC (NEP-141, `tusdc.rose-app.testnet`; Circle's NEAR testnet USDC exists but is only minted through a captcha-gated faucet, so it is not used). On Base, the same RoseVault authorization flow for the round's cash token.
- Position exit in kind: `redeem(position_id, to, nonce?)` burns `D_p` class units (the position's allocation) from the caller's free units (its accrued claimable stays with it) and authorizes the release of that position; the position is `Releasing` meanwhile. On Base the ERC-721 release (with the withdraw-first rule above), final once signed. On Solana the stream must carry no unconfirmed payout, and the caller also pays, from its own claimable tokens of the class, what the stream still owes the unit holders and takes away with it: what the position's units accrued at the class fraction since their checkpoint minus what payouts withdrew from the stream since its registration (at most `unlocked(now) - engine_withdrawn`; the rest of its unlocked tokens is the class fraction's rounding and margin, which no unit can claim; a holder that kept the position's units has at least that much claimable, because its holding accrues the floor of its exact accrual (section 4), so dust never blocks a sole holder's redeem, property-tested over random schedules and claim sequences; `get_sol_streams` reports the amount; refused when the caller's claimable, pending lien excess included, is short); the transaction is `AdvanceNonceAccount` + `transfer_recipient(vault -> to)` to `to`'s existing token account (proven with the nonce) + the receipt's `AdvanceNonceAccount`, with no withdrawal; a stream within five minutes of its end is not redeemed (Streamflow's auto-claim empties it at the end, after which `transfer_recipient` fails with error 104, "Stream closed", measured on devnet; its tokens are claimable anyway). It stays `Signed` until anyone calls `confirm_sol_redeem(position, anchor_tx, nonce_data, receipt_data)`, which settles it on the receipt exactly as `confirm_sol_payout` does: the receipt on the nonce's new value proves the release executed and the burn is final (`Released`); the receipt behind it, on two proofs 150 blocks apart, proves it did not, and the units come back to the redeemer (accruing from the burn, the stream having kept unlocking at the vault) with the claimable tokens it paid, and the position is `Held` again. The stream's own state takes no part: a new recipient can send the stream back to the vault, and the auto-claim closes it either way. Liens and units locked in rounds cannot be redeemed, and one release per position at a time; a fully withdrawn stream is not released; payouts never draw on a releasing stream.

### 7.4 Trust model (README must state it plainly)

- NEAR MPC network: threshold signing and CKD in TEEs; collusion of a threshold of nodes breaks custody and sealing. Foreign state is trusted to the MPC nodes' RPC quorum.
- rose-engine: keyless after init (verifiable: `view_access_key_list` returns `[]`), no upgrade or migrate method, no delete-account path, no generic call/execute method, CKD request only after the deadline. New versions deploy to new accounts.
- Gas: every user-facing method stays under 300 Tgas (wallet cap); the 1 PGas protocol limit is used only by scripts and the in-app demo wallet.
- Streamflow: upgradeable, source not public; its auto-claim withdraws every ended stream into its recipient's token account one second after the end (signer `wdrwhnCv4pzW8beKsbPa4S2UDZrXenjg16KJdKSpb5u`, memo "Auto-Claim", whatever `automatic_withdrawal` says). rose books it (`sync_sol_stream`, 7.3) and relies on it to reach an ended stream's last tranche: draws stop five minutes before the end.
- Tokens: on Base, an ERC-20 whose issuer keeps pause or blacklist powers can freeze RoseVault's balance or its transfers to a claimant; that is a trust assumption on the issuer, which rose cannot remove; the README states it. On Solana, mints with a freeze authority are refused at deposit (7.2), so no third party can freeze the vault's token account.
- RoseVault: immutable, no owner, no pause, no upgrade, no `receive`; lockup and signer fixed in the constructor. A stream sent to it with a plain `transferFrom` (bypassing `depositStream`) is unattributed and can only leave through a Release authorization; the docs say so.
- No other party can move funds, see bids early or stop a round.

## 8. Frontend

### 8.1 Visual direction

Dark, ember accent, dithered voxel aesthetic ("dither / cubism, Minecraft-like"). Palette: near-black background, warm greys, ember orange and rose red accents; ordered (Bayer) dithering for gradients, glows and illustrations; voxel iconography. Typography: a pixel or bitmap display face for headings (open license, e.g. Departure Mono or Pixelify Sans, pick after testing legibility), a clean sans for body, a mono face for numbers. The app itself is clean and data-dense (reference quality: Hyperliquid, Linear, Nasdaq Private Market); the dither and voxel language is used for accents, empty states, illustrations and loading states, never at the cost of legibility. Target viewport: 1470 px wide MacBook; fully responsive down to 375 px.

### 8.2 Landing (scroll-driven, three.js + post-processing dither)

A pinned scroll sequence (~400 vh), rendered in WebGL with a final ordered-dither pass; the phrase is rendered in the scene so it receives the same lighting and dithering (accessible DOM copy for screen readers and SEO).

1. Black screen. The phrase "What was locked, rose." sits in deep shadow, barely legible. Only the letters of "rose" smolder like embers.
2. Scrolling, a voxel dragon slowly emerges behind the phrase, eyes first.
3. The dragon breathes fire in pulses driven by scroll. The fire washes over the phrase; each letter lights up as the flame reaches it, and "rose" glows brighter and brighter.
4. The phrase grows and leaves through the top of the screen.
5. The dragon falls back asleep: only smoke drifting from its mouth and the tip of its tail still glowing.
6. Hovering the dragon or the glowing tail reveals a "Launch app" button (also reachable by keyboard focus, always visible on touch devices, and in the top navigation).
7. Below the sequence: a short "How rose works" strip (Tender, Exit, Advance in one line each), live protocol stats read from the contract, and a footer with Docs, GitHub, Contracts, Status.

Requirements: 60 fps on an M-series MacBook, adaptive quality on weaker GPUs, `prefers-reduced-motion` shows a static final frame with the button, the voxel dragon is authored by us (procedural or hand-authored voxel data, no third-party model licensing issue), total landing payload kept small and lazy-loaded.

### 8.3 App information architecture (every element functional, no placeholder)

Global: top bar (logo, Rounds, Portfolio, Issuer, Prices, Docs, network badge "Testnet", connected wallets NEAR / EVM / Solana with balances, Connect button), testnet banner with a "Get test funds" panel, toasts with explorer links, a global activity feed, contextual "Learn more" links into the docs, first-visit guided tour (dismissable), command palette.

- **Portfolio** (`/app`): units per class with locked and claimable amounts, unlock timelines, open bids and asks, liens (as holder and as funder), claimable cash and tokens per chain with "Claim" buttons, history with explorer links.
- **Rounds** (`/app/rounds`): filterable list (type, class, status), countdowns, round cards, and a "Create Exit round" action (class, window, cash venue; `create_exit` is open to anyone).
- **Round detail** (`/app/rounds/[id]`): public parameters, status timeline with the next action button for the current user, sealed book (count of sealed bids and their public collateral, ciphertext hashes, nothing else), "Place bid" or "Place ask" form with live fee and collateral preview, my bids, and after close: key release event, opening progress, supply and demand curves animating into the clearing price, fills table, settlement and payout links on NEAR, Base and Solana. A "Verify" panel lets anyone recompute the clearing price in the browser from the opened bids.
- **Deposit** (`/app/deposit`): wizard: choose chain, list eligible streams, explain eligibility failures, approve and deposit, live proof progress (MPC verification), units minted.
- **Issuer console** (`/app/issuer`): create a class from a stream or schedule, create the cohort's streams in custody (`createStreams`, one transaction, one `StreamDeposited` per holder) and register them, distribute units (`distribute_units`, bounded batch with NEP-245 events), launch Tender (budget, max price or max discount, window), fund it (NEAR USDC or Base USDC), "Run via DAO" that builds a Sputnik DAO function-call proposal (and a Trezu custom template) for treasuries that need approval, results page with an exportable tender report (CSV and printable page).

- **Status** (`/status`): engine account keyless check, contract versions, MPC latency of recent requests (from the on-chain event history timestamps), RoseVault, Solana vault and `gas/sol` balances, recent ForeignTx verifications.

Every async action has loading, success and failure states, human-readable errors (wallet rejected, insufficient balance, MPC timeout with retry, expired blockhash with retry), and an explorer link. Empty states explain what to do next.
